1. Run your first study

  1. In File Explorer, open AeroDesk-win32-x64/AeroDesk.exe from the extracted release folder. An EXE cannot run inside a document preview. Configure the separate solver installation using RUNTIME.md.
  2. Choose New project → Create blank, then import a model using the left sidebar. The app starts with a blank project.
  3. Check the real longest dimension in meters. For a plane, the nose faces −X, the top faces +Y, and wings span Z in the solver view.
  4. Choose the intended speed, air properties and angle of attack. Keep OpenFOAM · 3D RANS selected for physical aerodynamic results.
  5. Choose Draft, Standard, Detailed or Reference quality and leave CPU workers on Auto. Press Run. There is no time cutoff: the solver checks residuals, all forces and moments, with an iteration limit to prevent an endless run.
  6. Inspect convergence and mesh quality before interpreting the loads. Compare a finer mesh at the same settings.
  7. Save the project. Export the PDF report or native results when you want to share an analysis.
Projects store geometry, settings, material, and every queued/completed angle condition. Full sampled 3D fields and wall loads are saved in a companion your-project.aero.results folder. Keep it beside the .aero file when moving or sharing a project. Reopening restores the selected result without another solve. The original native OpenFOAM mesh and case remain separate on this PC.

2. Import and clean models

FormatWhat AeroDesk does
STL / OBJLoads triangle geometry. These files do not reliably specify units: enter the real size. OBJ parts retain their shared placement.
STEP / STPUses OpenCascade to tessellate CAD solids. Declared units become meters, assembly placement is retained, and CAD Z-up is made upright.
3MFReads build objects, component transforms and declared units, including production-extension component references. CAD Z-up is made upright. Mesh geometry is supported; beam-only and slice-only models require a mesh export.

Import one assembly file at a time, under 32 MB and 200,000 triangles after tessellation. If CAD exports incorrect unit metadata, correct the displayed physical size before running.

Cleanup removes invalid or duplicate triangles, joins tiny seams, repairs small holes and fixes winding. Overlapping closed parts are joined into an exterior solid where possible. Parts that do not overlap are kept separate. The original CAD file is unchanged.

Read the cleanup summary. Union removes internal contact surfaces. Review narrow gaps, thin wings and deliberately open ducts. A part with large openings, non-manifold edges or self-intersections may need CAD repair; the app will not silently pretend that an invalid mesh is suitable for CFD. If conservative repair fails, AeroDesk attempts an approximate exterior rebuild at 384 cells across the longest dimension. Expand the cleanup report to inspect it. Features or gaps smaller than about two cells can change; for a 1.37 m model this is roughly 7 mm. Compare the repaired shape with CAD, especially wing tips, thin trailing edges and control gaps. This does not establish CFD accuracy. The Rebuild exterior button repeats this repair explicitly. Saving a cleaned STL uses meters.

3. CAD connections

Onshape

  1. Click Import from Onshape. Connect a read-only Onshape API key in the app.
  2. Choose the document, workspace and Part Studio. Select a specific exterior solid or the entire Part Studio.
  3. Import. Onshape’s +Z-up is converted to the viewer’s upright +Y direction. Verify the nose direction and real size.
  4. After CAD edits, choose Refresh from Onshape. This replaces the imported model and clears old results.

Use workspace links to follow edits; version links stay fixed. Named CAD assemblies are not imported directly through the Onshape picker; use a Part Studio or an exported STEP assembly. Your Onshape document is never edited by cleanup.

Autodesk Fusion

  1. In AeroDesk click Connect Autodesk Fusion → Install / update Fusion add-in.
  2. In Fusion’s Design workspace, open Utilities → Scripts and Add-Ins → Add-Ins. Select AeroDeskLink, click Run and enable Run on Startup.
  3. With your design open, choose Send to AeroDesk in Fusion’s Utilities panel. It exports the active design locally as STEP.
  4. Back in AeroDesk, click Connect Autodesk Fusion → Import latest design. Repeat export/import after CAD changes.

This connection uses the Fusion account already signed in on your PC. It needs no additional cloud API key. It is a local export bridge, not a live two-way CAD editor.

4. Move around the model

  • Drag: orbit. Mouse wheel: zoom. Right drag: pan.
  • View cube: click a face for an orthogonal view. “All six views” exposes faces currently facing away. Iso shows an isometric view.
  • Fit: top-right only; frames the model. Escape also resets the view when you are not editing a field.
  • Focus: top-right only; hides the setup sidebar for more viewer space. Click ← Setup to restore it. Opening the app does not enable Focus automatically.
  • Wind arrow: stays near the model’s lower level and points along airflow. It indicates flow direction. Enabling Ground / seabed adds a separate physical wall below the model.
  • CG / Lift markers: small purple and blue markers. CG uses a uniform solid estimate unless you enter a measured CG. The blue marker and vertical arrow show the signed vertical lift distribution. A hollow marker means convergence or numerical quality checks are incomplete. The Balance & lift panel shows each wing side’s lift.

Pressure, skin friction, flow speed, streamlines, slices, vortices and pressure clouds use the solved fields. Vortex colors show speed. In Streamlines, select Vertical or Horizontal, then drag Plane position to move the seed plane live. The coordinate readout follows the slider. Traces follow the actual 3D flow and can bend out of that plane. All shows streamlines seeded across the whole model. Drag the slice slider for continuous movement. Automatic slice contrast uses a locked near-model range with a minimum span of 10% of inlet speed; fixed velocity colors use the inlet reference. Smoother colors do not make the CFD mesh more accurate.

Quality levels and reliability

ModePurposeWhat changes
DraftScreen ideasCoarser mesh, four requested wall layers, relaxed numerical targets. No plane score.
StandardBalanced studyFiner surfaces, ten requested layers, near-wall spacing estimate and tighter convergence.
DetailedMore detailFine surfaces, fourteen requested layers and stricter force/moment stability.
ReferenceSeveral hours or overnightLess diffusive velocity numerics, three successively finer meshes, sixteen requested layers, strict convergence and explicit force/moment comparison.

Duration depends on the geometry, CPU/GPU and convergence. Iteration limits are 2,500 / 6,000 / 9,000 / 12,000 per mesh; reaching one without meeting the checks produces a result marked for review. Reference is allowed to run for three hours or more; the app never waits artificially to make a result appear more accurate. Changing the hardware changes the estimated duration, not the requested quality. Insufficient memory produces an explanation rather than silently lowering resolution.

Open Quality to inspect convergence, independent surface-load agreement, boundary flow balance, actual layer coverage, area-weighted wall y+, and the mesh comparison. Requested layers can fail to grow on tight corners or narrow gaps. Coverage is checked before solving, with an early warning when it is poor. In Display controls choose Boundary-layer count to see the actual layer counts mapped onto CAD; red marks missing layers. Unknown legacy counts remain gray. For smooth walls, the resolved modes check that at least 90% of the surface has y+ ≤ 2. Rough surfaces use wall-function treatment instead. The saved boundary flux must balance within 0.001% of incoming flow, with solid-wall leakage below 0.000001%. This is a global conservation check; it does not prove local accuracy. These are screening thresholds, not a universal accuracy guarantee.

Reference compares all three native force and moment vectors. It requires each mesh to converge and pass wall-layer/y+ and independent load checks, meaningful cell-count growth, and agreement within stated relative limits (2% forces, 3% moments, with absolute floors near zero). Where net lift is well determined, the lift location must also change by less than 0.5% of model length. This is a mesh-sensitivity test, not a formal grid-convergence index or wind-tunnel validation. All native cases remain on this PC under the study folder. If a later mesh fails or is stopped, the last completed result is retained and marked incomplete.

A rebuilt exterior is an approximation to your CAD. Smaller CFD cells cannot recover geometry already lost during repair. Preserve original CAD and compare thin wings, control gaps and trailing edges before using the results.

Air, water and ground

In Set your flow, choose Water · fully submerged for a body entirely underwater. This applies freshwater at 20°C: density 998.2 kg/m³ and kinematic viscosity 1.004 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s. Switching back to Air restores 1.225 kg/m³ and 1.5 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s. Speed and model size stay unchanged; Reynolds number updates. Advanced fluid settings remain editable for other temperatures or fluids. Loading a project preserves its saved properties.

This is single-phase incompressible steady RANS. The water is treated as extending above the model: there is no waterline, wave, floating equilibrium, cavitation or hydrostatic buoyancy calculation. Lift and drag are flow-induced forces, not total immersed-body loads. No aircraft flight score is assigned to water runs.

Set Ground / seabed to On · moving with flow for a vehicle or submerged body travelling above a stationary ground or seabed. In the fixed-model reference frame the ground moves at the inlet velocity. Choose On · stationary tunnel floor for a model held above a fixed tunnel floor; its inlet-to-model boundary-layer growth affects the result. Off restores an open-space domain.

Clearance is the vertical distance from the lowest point of the rotated model to the ground. Zero means contact; there is no artificial gap. Very small gaps and contact regions can be hard to mesh. Check native mesh quality and wall resolution. The floor is smooth even when model roughness is set. In angle studies, clearance stays constant at each angle, so the model's center height can change. These runs are excluded from the free-flight plane score; force comparisons remain available.

The floor changes the actual CFD domain and wall conditions, and is drawn in the viewer. Near-body ground y+ and layer coverage are checked separately from the model. Draft remains screening quality. Higher modes cannot pass numerical quality if the ground wall checks fail. Compare more than one clearance and mesh for ground-effect work. Fluid, floor type and clearance are saved with every study and project.

Preset reference: Water at 20°C properties. Boundary-condition reference: OpenFOAM wall conditions.

5. Understand the simulation

AeroDesk runs steady incompressible OpenFOAM simpleFoam with the k–ω SST turbulence model. This is a time-averaged solution, not a transient flow animation. Transition, compressibility, rotating propellers and dynamic aircraft motion are not simulated.

  • Reynolds number: speed × longest dimension ÷ kinematic viscosity. It is displayed with three significant figures. Changing Re adjusts speed.
  • Reference area: affects coefficients, not the force in newtons. Full-model projection includes the body and tail; it is not measured main-wing area.
  • Material density: estimates the mass of a uniform solid. It cannot infer battery, motor, shell or payload mass.
  • Convergence: inspect both residuals and force trends. A small residual does not prove mesh independence.
  • CPU / GPU: MPI uses multiple CPU workers for solving. Meshing and export remain serial. With a compatible runtime installed, choose Compute → GPU · CUDA pressure solver to use a supported NVIDIA GPU through WSL Ubuntu, OpenFOAM v2512 and PETSc CUDA. Pressure matrix/vector work uses the GPU; momentum, turbulence, meshing and export use the CPU. CPU and GPU are separate choices. Small meshes may be faster on CPU. GPU native cases are stored in your user home/AeroDeskGPU/cases. The Linux runtime must be set up separately on each PC; it is not inside the portable EXE.

6. Plane assessment and better design decisions

Open the Plane tab yourself when you want an assessment. Automatic shape recognition is a proportion/outline screen; use “This is a plane” if it misses your model.

A score is withheld when convergence or recorded numerical quality checks fail. When available, the provisional score compares L/D to your target, lift to actual flying weight, aerodynamic pitch trim at measured CG, and left/right lift balance. Missing checks are excluded and coverage is shown. Read the score method before comparing designs. Keep targets, flight conditions and coverage the same.

Locate problems

Choose Show numbered regions. D1–D3 identify spatial groups of wall faces with large net drag. T1–T3 identify nearby air regions with high modeled turbulent kinetic energy k. Read the locations and values. A high-k region is not proof that the nearest part caused it; inspect upstream geometry and compare a changed design at matched lift.

Measure static responses

  1. Enter your measured CG, including battery and installed equipment, in meters from the model center.
  2. Run a baseline at zero yaw and roll.
  3. Choose Run four stability studies. It runs pitch −3°/+3° about the baseline and yaw −3°/+3°, each using your quality level. Stop cancels the sequence.
  4. Read restoring/destabilizing tendencies only when both paired runs converge and the response exceeds final-iteration variation. Otherwise the result is inconclusive.
  5. Try the suggested change—such as tail volume or dihedral—and rerun at the same settings, with a finer mesh and another perturbation size for confirmation.
The sideslip roll response is dihedral effect, not complete roll stability. Spiral and Dutch-roll behavior also need inertia and dynamic derivatives. A single lift-line position cannot establish stability. The score does not certify flight readiness.

With multiple saved conditions, the combined score uses all checked, matched, converged cases. It weights mean condition score 60%, pitch restoring response 16%, sideslip roll response 12%, and directional response 12%. Unknown criteria are excluded and coverage is shown. This is a transparent comparison rubric, not an absolute aircraft rating. Compare identical angle lists, targets, CG, mass and flow settings.

7. Optional AI design adviser

  1. Open Plane → Connect AI / settings. Enter an OpenAI API key directly in the app. Do not share your key in a project or with friends.
  2. The app verifies the key and stores it using Windows encryption. An OPENAI_API_KEY environment variable is used automatically if present when the app launches.
  3. Choose an available API model. Click Review this evidence with AI.
  4. AeroDesk sends the current numerical condition, score inputs, quality flags, spatial findings and completed sweep findings and selected angle-study summaries. It does not send the CAD file or Onshape/Fusion credentials.
  5. Review suggestions critically and test them. AI does not run missing CFD tests or edit your CAD model.

API usage is billed to your API account. This Codex/ChatGPT conversation cannot automatically supply an API key. No request is sent until you ask for an AI review. “Remove saved key” deletes the encrypted local key; an environment key remains active until removed from the environment.

9. Display & design advice · 0.12

Velocity slices default to automatic contrast calculated near the model. Their range stays locked while dragging and uses a minimum span of 10% of inlet speed to avoid exaggerating tiny variations. Blue is slower; orange/red is faster. Far from the model, nearly uniform flow should still look uniform. Choose Full / reference scale or Manual when comparing different studies.

Fine display refines vortex and pressure isosurfaces by interpolation with smooth normals. This does not add CFD resolution. Increase the Q threshold to isolate stronger vortex structures; lower it to see more weak structures. Vortices are colored by speed. Fast display is available for slower PCs.

Center of lift uses the signed vertical component of pressure and friction on every native wall face. Its vertical line is drawn at CG height; changing that height does not shift its front–back or sideways location. Drag and side-force moments are retained separately under Combined force axis. The lift point is not the aerodynamic center, and it can lie outside the wing when loads oppose each other. Unequal wing-side loads move it sideways. Check the model geometry, yaw/roll, convergence and mesh resolution before interpreting that offset as a real aerodynamic effect. The app never snaps it to the centerline. Use a measured CG and converged pitch/sideslip studies for stability investigation.

Open Plane assessment and select a Design advice card to highlight a measured region. Each card separates evidence, a candidate CAD experiment and the comparison to run. No inward/outward displacement or drag saving is predicted: those require a separate adjoint or geometry-variation solve.

All controls remain available under Display controls, Mesh & grid, Forces, Quality and Export & native data. New project is only in the header. Close the advice annotation with its × button.

10. Many angles, one saved project

  1. Import your plane, confirm nose −X / up +Y / span Z, real size, speed, air properties and reference area.
  2. Click Angle studies above the viewer. Enter comma-separated AoA and yaw lists, and a quality level per case. Without the combination checkbox, AoA varies at yaw 0 and yaw varies at AoA 0. With it, every AoA × yaw combination runs (up to 49 at once).
  3. Add conditions builds a queue. Run queued studies runs them one after another. Auto assessment · 9 angles adds and runs AoA −4, −2, 0, 2, 4, 6, 8° at zero yaw, plus yaw −5, +5° at zero AoA. Each condition must meet its own convergence checks. Reference quality solves three meshes for every angle; large batches can take a day or longer.
  4. The quality and compute selection at Run apply to that run; stored physical conditions stay unchanged. Stop queue cancels the active case and preserves completed results. Run queued resumes remaining cases. The ↻ next to a completed result reruns just that condition.
  5. Click View to switch the model and 3D field to that condition. Compare lift, drag, L/D or pitch moment in the chart. Hollow points mark unconverged results. Checkboxes select conditions for the combined score; cases at different speed, scale, fluid or reference area are excluded from the current comparison.
  6. Enter actual flying mass and measured CG before interpreting trim or stability. Adjacent matched converged angles provide static response slopes; noise-dominated responses remain inconclusive. The app does not infer stall, dynamic stability or safe flight from these sweeps.
  7. Click Save project & results. Save after a batch or before closing. Reopen the .aero file; keep its .aero.results folder alongside it. If a companion result is missing, the condition remains listed and can be rerun.

11. Materials and cleaner viewer

Material & center of gravity contains 186 presets from the official Onshape material library, with density and listed mechanical properties. Selecting a preset sets solid density. Import/refresh reads the material assigned in Onshape automatically when every selected part has the same usable material. Mixed or unassigned parts are reported; no single density is invented. Mechanical properties are shown for reference; they do not turn CFD into a structural analysis. Uniform density cannot locate batteries or account for hollow construction.

Drag the view cube to orbit it, then click a visible face to look normal to that face. Fit and Focus remain at the top right. Purple marks CG; blue marks lift, without floating text labels. Their coordinates and front–back separation appear in Balance & lift. Nose is −X, so negative body-X separation means the aerodynamic marker is ahead of CG. The force-convergence and spanwise-lift plots expand under the viewer. A provisional lift center can move with convergence and mesh quality; it is not forced onto an expected wing location.
